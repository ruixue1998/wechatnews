💡 Sam Altman：社会应接受 AI 带来的部分负面事件

OpenAI CEO Sam Altman recently stated in an interview with Politico's "Decoded" podcast that the world should accept some negative events brought by AI in exchange for technological benefits and user autonomy. OpenAI CEO 萨姆·奥尔特曼近日接受 Politico 播客《Decoded》采访时表示，世界应接受 AI 带来的部分负面事件，以换取技术收益和用户自主权。

He said that one of the disagreements between OpenAI and some stricter AI safety advocates is the belief that "some bad things will happen" as society explores system resilience, and that regulation should take a lighter approach. 他说，OpenAI 与部分更严格的 AI 安全倡导者的分歧之一，是认为社会在探索系统韧性时「一些不好的事情将会发生」，监管应采取更轻的方式。

This statement was made during a discussion about the differences in AI safety approaches between OpenAI and Anthropic. 这段表态是在讨论 OpenAI 与 Anthropic 的 AI 安全路线差异时提出的。

Altman believes that the benefits brought by AI will outweigh the harms by orders of magnitude; however, Gary Marcus, an honorary professor at New York University, criticized this argument, saying it turns social risks into the cost of corporate expansion. Both positions revolve around one question: once models are more widely open, which risks can society bear, and which need to be blocked before release? 奥特曼认为，AI 带来的好处数量级上会超过坏处；纽约大学荣誉教授 Gary Marcus 则批评这套说法把社会风险转成了公司扩张的代价。两种立场都围绕一个问题展开：模型被更广泛开放后，哪些风险可以由社会承担，哪些需要在发布前被挡住。

Altman's focus is on placing regulatory boundaries on system resilience and user choice: if risks can be discovered, explained, and fixed during use, he tends to let products enter real environments first; if risks can cause irreversible harm, restrictions should be set before release. 奥特曼的重点是把监管边界放在系统韧性和用户选择上：如果风险可以被发现、解释并在使用中修复，他倾向于让产品先进入真实环境；如果风险会造成不可逆伤害，则应在发布前设置限制。

Marcus's rebuttal, however, focuses on accountability, arguing that treating "some bad things will happen" as the cost of innovation might make affected individuals bear losses that should be borne by companies and regulators. Marcus 的反驳则集中在责任归属，认为把「会发生一些坏事」当成创新成本，可能让受影响的人承担本应由公司和监管者承担的损失。
