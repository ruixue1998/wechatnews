苹果详解 iPhone 18 Pro 隐私提示点，确认亮起后才向系统放行音视频

🔗 security.apple.com/blog/secure-exclaves

Apple's security research team publicly released the design of Secure Exclaves on November 12. Apple stated that in background sensing mode, raw data from cameras and microphones remains in an independent operating environment, and the main system only receives limited information such as recognition results. 苹果安全研究团队 11 月 12 日公开了 Secure Exclaves 的设计。苹果称，在后台感知模式下，摄像头和麦克风的原始数据留在独立的运行环境里，主系统只能收到识别结果等有限信息。

Taking "Hey Siri" on the iPhone 18 Pro as an example, the low-power coprocessor first identifies potential wake words, then hands it over to the isolated environment for secondary confirmation. At this point, the main system only receives the wake signal. After the microphone indicator dot on the screen is checked for color, shape, and display duration, the isolated environment then passes the audio containing the wake word and user request to Siri. 以 iPhone 18 Pro 的「嘿 Siri」为例，低功耗协处理器先识别可能的唤醒词，再交给隔离环境二次确认。此时主系统只会收到唤醒信号。屏幕上的麦克风提示点通过颜色、形状和显示时长检查后，隔离环境才会把包含唤醒词和用户请求的音频交给 Siri。

Apple stated that this architecture is also used for screen privacy prompts on the iPhone 18 Pro Max and iPhone Duo, as well as person recognition in HomeView and camera prompts on the MacBook Neo. 苹果称，这套架构也用于 iPhone 18 Pro Max 和 iPhone Duo 的屏幕隐私提示，以及 HomeView 的人物识别和 MacBook Neo 的摄像头提示。

前字节、美团招聘负责人魏小康谈 AI 人才泡沫：高薪要看能解决什么问题

In an interview with "LatePost," Wei Xiaokang, who previously helped build ByteDance's recruitment system and was responsible for Meituan's recruitment, said that when measuring the compensation of large model talent, one should first look at what problems they can solve. Training foundational models requires a large amount of computing power, and decisions made by a few core researchers can affect the efficiency of training resource utilization, and even determine whether a model can be successfully developed. 在《晚点 LatePost》的访谈中，曾参与搭建字节跳动招聘体系、负责过美团招聘的魏小康说，衡量大模型人才的薪酬，应该先看他们能解决什么问题。基座模型训练需要大量算力，少数核心研究者做的决定，会影响训练资源的使用效率，甚至决定模型能不能做出来。

Wei Xiaokang recalled that the early "hundred-model war" drove up researchers' salaries, with some companies spending high prices to form teams, only for the recruited individuals to be unable to complete large model development. He believes that this type of investment constitutes a talent bubble.
